# L5: The Bipotent Cell

L5 is the cell that can become *two* cell types. After
L4 (oligopotent, ~4 fates) comes L5 (bipotent, exactly
2 fates). The classic: the granulocyte-monocyte progenitor
(GMP) makes either a granulocyte or a monocyte, but not
both lineages.

L5 is the *last branch point*. Once a cell crosses L5 →
L6, the choice is made. The L5 cell sits at the fork; the
fork decides based on niche signals (cytokines, growth
factors, ECM).

**The math:** `P(fate_A) = k_A / (k_A + k_B)`. For the GMP:
G-CSF → granulocyte (k_G ≈ 1.0), M-CSF → monocyte
(k_M ≈ 0.7). P(granulocyte) ≈ 0.59, P(monocyte) ≈ 0.41.

In Quilt: a L5 cell is a L4 cell whose BIND signature has
narrowed to two possibilities. The CRDT merge with the
niche is the *decision* — the L5 cell receives two conflicting
signals and resolves by sampling.

**The 4 gold terms:**

1. **Bipotent Fork** — the L5 signature. Two arrows, two fates.
2. **GMP Decision** — the canonical L5 example.
3. **Stochastic Resolution** — noisy. Even with identical
   signals, sister L5 cells may pick differently (~10-20%
   stochastic component).
4. **Cytokine Bias** — the niche shifts fork weights.
   Inflammation biases toward granulocytes; chronic injury
   biases toward monocytes.

**The 3 analogies:**

1. **L5 = a fork in the road.** Two paths, two fates.
2. **GMP Decision = a college student choosing a major.**
3. **Stochastic Resolution = a coin flip in the wind.**

**The cowboy's sentence:**

> The cowboy came to a fork in the trail. One trail led
> to the high country; one trail led to the low country.
> The cowboy looked at the wind, looked at the sun,
> listened to the grass, and chose. The cowboy called
> the fork *L5*.
