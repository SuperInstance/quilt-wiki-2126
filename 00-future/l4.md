# L4: The Oligopotent Cell

L4 is the cell that can become a *few* cell types within a
single lineage. After L3 (multipotent, ~10 fates) comes L4
(oligopotent, ~4 fates in one progenitor). Examples: the
common myeloid progenitor (CMP) makes granulocytes,
monocytes, erythrocytes — but not lymphocytes.

**The math:** `H(L4) = -sum p_i * log_2(p_i)`. For a uniform
4-fate progenitor: H = 2 bits. The L-tier progression is
*literally* the bit-budget of cellular identity, halving
roughly each step (L3 ~ 3.3 bits, L4 = 2, L5 = 1, L6 = 0).

**The 4 gold terms:**

1. **Lineage Funnel** — the narrowing of fates as the cell
   divides. HSC at the top, neutrophil at the bottom.
2. **Common Progenitor** — the L4 cell. CMP, CLP, etc.
3. **Bipotent Restriction** — the L4-to-L5 transition. The
   cell's BIND signature narrows from 4 to 2 fates.
4. **Fate-Mapping Confidence** — the probability that an
   L4 cell's daughter is the *expected* cell type (~85-95%
   in vivo for most progenitors).

**The 3 analogies:**

1. **L4 = a college major.** Required courses (fates).
2. **Lineage Funnel = a water filter.** Narrows at each step.
3. **Bipotent Restriction = a fork in the road.** Before the
   fork, many places; after, only two.

**The cowboy's sentence:**

> The cowboy rode the L3 trail and chose a herd. The
> cowboy rode the L4 trail and chose a pen in the herd.
