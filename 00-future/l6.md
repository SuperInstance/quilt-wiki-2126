# L6: The Determined Cell

L6 is the cell that has made its *final* fate choice — but
can still be reprogrammed. L6 ≠ L9: L6 is "determined but
reprogrammable"; L9 is "specialized and locked." L6 cells
have *latent plasticity* that L9 cells have lost.

The clearest proof: Takahashi & Yamanaka (2006) showed that
mouse embryonic fibroblasts (L6) can be reprogrammed to L1
(iPSC) with just 4 factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc). The
L6 cell's BIND signature is *overwritable*.

**The math:** `R(L6) = p_OSKM * (1-p_senescence) * (1-p_apoptosis)`.
Typical mouse in vitro: p_OSKM ≈ 0.01-0.10, p_senescence ≈ 0.20-0.40,
p_apoptosis ≈ 0.10-0.20. Net R(L6) ≈ 0.005-0.05 (0.5-5%).

In Quilt: a L6 cell is a L5 cell that has resolved its fork.
BIND is single-fate; PROOF seals can be *re-opened* by the
OSKM factors. The journal still records the L5 → L6
transition; the ring is "sealed but not witnessed."

**The 4 gold terms:**

1. **Yamanaka Cocktail** — Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc. Reprograms L6 → L1.
2. **Latent Plasticity** — capacity to be reprogrammed; lost in L9.
3. **Pre-iPSC** — partial reprogramming; lost some L6, gained some L1.
4. **Reprogramming Memory** — epigenetic memory of original fate;
   fades over passages, rarely zero.

**The 3 analogies:**

1. **L6 = a college graduate.** Committed to a major, but the
   career can still be changed.
2. **Yamanaka Cocktail = a 4-week sabbatical.**
3. **Reprogramming Memory = an accent.** Fades but never zero.

**The cowboy's sentence:**

> The cowboy's horse was a ranch horse. The cowboy trained
> it for the trail. The horse was committed, but the ranch
> was still in the muscle. The cowboy could take the horse
> back to the ranch (Yamanaka); the horse would remember
> the trail. The cowboy called the horse *L6*.
